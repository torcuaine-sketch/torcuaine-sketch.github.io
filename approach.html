How we invest

We invest according to a written investment policy agreed with each client: the objectives, the limits and who takes the decisions. Our research journal shows the same discipline applied to public market history.

The investment policy

Each portfolio follows its own written policy. It records what the portfolio is for, the risks it may take and the reporting the client receives. We manage within it and report against it.

How we work →

How we read markets

We classify instruments by how they behave: how each acts when a regime turns and what it moves with under stress. Every position starts as a written thesis with a test that can prove it wrong.

How we take decisions

We run a staged nightly cycle: regime, signals, blend, debate, optimisation, risk, validation. Views are blended through Black-Litterman and hierarchical risk parity and weighted by what the current regime supports.

Every allocation rule is validated on a rolling walk-forward window before it reaches production. Limits apply before a trade exists, and the drawdown protocol is agreed in advance.

The three strategies →

Reconstruction pricing and sources

Each reconstruction names its instrument, its window and its fills basis. Each entry pays the ask; each exit takes the bid. The full quoted spread counts as a cost in every result. Fills cross the quoted end-of-day chain. The pages build on adjusted exchange records and end-of-day option chains. Each one closes with the open questions.

Session-note sources

Each session note names its instrument, its date, its price source and its implied-volatility source. Prices are the adjusted daily closes; implied volatility comes from end-of-day chains.

Scope of the journal

The journal studies public market history. The reconstructions are after-the-fact studies, not a track record. The way we work with clients is on the how-we-work page.
