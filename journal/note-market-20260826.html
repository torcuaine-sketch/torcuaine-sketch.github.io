Market data note (multi-instrument), 26 August 2026

Market overview, 26 August 2026. A daily note from public exchange records and end-of-day option data, covering AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, TSLA.

Type
Market note
Date

The broad market was nearly unchanged on 26 August 2026 - the S&P 500 proxy rose 0.02% - yet the four covered mega-caps split two and two rather than moving as a bloc. Apple led the universe, rising 1.15% to close at 313.45, while Microsoft followed with a 0.95% advance to 496.37. On the other side, Nvidia fell 1.59% to 209.66 and Tesla declined 1.26% to 345.82. The dividing line was not sector membership in any simple sense, since three of the four names sit in Information Technology; what separated the session was participation. Nvidia traded at 1.53 times its 20-day average volume, the only name in the group with above-average turnover, while Apple, Microsoft, and Tesla all traded below their averages, at 0.70, 0.62, and 0.86 times respectively. The heaviest trading, in other words, occurred in the name doing the worst.

Apple and Microsoft moved in the same direction but for visibly different reasons, judging by their idiosyncratic residuals against peer baskets. Microsoft's session matched its software basket almost exactly - a residual of roughly zero with a 60-day z-score near 0.00 - making it effectively a pure group move. Apple's rise, by contrast, came against a consumer-electronics basket that fell about 1.06%, leaving a positive residual near 2.2% and a z-score of 1.17: firm, but not extreme, stock-specific strength. The 30-day drift measures separate the pair more sharply. Microsoft's drift z-score of 1.44 is the strongest in the universe, while Apple's at -1.37 is the weakest, meaning the session's leader has been the month's laggard and vice versa. Apple's sector-rotation field is null, so that observation is unavailable; Microsoft's software group shows 21-day median excess of 2.96 against -1.22 over 10 days.

Nvidia was the session's clear outlier, and the divergence is striking given its news flow. The data show zero days to earnings, and coverage of the report was previewed during the session without figures. Earlier items were directionally positive: a $20 billion acquisition of Groq and reported warnings to customers of price increases exceeding 15% on AI servers. A lower-materiality item noted an OpenAI custom chip beating Nvidia on select inference-efficiency tests. The stock fell anyway, posting a residual of roughly -3.2% against a semiconductor basket that rose about 1.6%, a 60-day z-score of -1.28. Group context partly explains it: the semiconductor cohort's 10-day median excess stands at -5.07, though no formal rotation flag is set. Nvidia also sits furthest from its 20-day high at -7.2%. Its implied volatility of 34.68% carries an IV rank of only 20.8%, so options pricing does not register unusual stress relative to the past year.

Tesla completed the declining half, but unlike Nvidia it moved with its basket rather than against it: a -1.26% session versus a basket near -0.99%, a residual z-score of -0.10. Its consumer-discretionary group shows 21-day median excess of -7.8 alongside a 10-day reading of 1.34, suggesting the cohort's earlier weakness has recently stalled. Tesla carries the universe's highest implied volatility at 40.25% yet the lowest IV rank at 16.4%, echoing Nvidia's pattern of elevated absolute vol with low relative pricing. Positioning against 20-day highs orders the group neatly: Microsoft at -1.91% is closest, followed by Tesla at -4.7%, Apple at -5.99%, and Nvidia at -7.2%. Taken together, the session was idiosyncratic rather than macro - a flat index, two quiet basket-tracking moves in opposite directions, one idiosyncratic Apple advance, and one heavily traded Nvidia decline into its earnings event.
