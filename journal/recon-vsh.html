VSH - historical reconstruction, 30 Mar to 18 Jun 2026

Vishay Intertechnology (VSH), 30 March to 18 June 2026. A reconstruction of a structural break in a neglected cyclical component maker, assembled from dated exchange records and end-of-day option quotations.

Type
Reconstruction
Window
30 Mar - 18 Jun 2026
Instrument
VSH Jul-2026 monthly 22.5 call
Fills basis
End-of-day chains, crossed

Over the window VSH rose 291.9 per cent on adjusted closes, from 16.56 to 64.90. The note weighs what in the move carried dates beforehand and how the market quoted one defined-risk option structure along the way.

Observed before

What was knowable beforehand

The entry case rests entirely on facts dated 9 April 2026 or earlier - the session the reconstruction takes as its entry point. The 52-week reference level of 20.74 stood public from 3 February. The panic low of 16.30 on 30 March and the V-reversal of 31 March (+8.7 per cent, close 18.00) sat in the record. The 8 April gap to 20.49 on 2.6 million shares put the stock back at the February top, and the 9 April intraday break above 20.74 - high 21.55, close 21.33 - is the signal; in an end-of-day replay, the close of the breakout session is the trigger.

The track held a dated catalyst further ahead. The fourth-quarter report of 4 February had shown a revenue surprise of +0.9 per cent - a stabilising top line against a miss on earnings - and the quarterly cadence placed the first-quarter report in mid-May. It in fact arrived on 13 May, with an earnings surprise of +66.7 per cent and an 8 per cent gap. The insider tape before entry ran clean: RSU grants on 25 February and tax-withholding F-coded sales on 2 March at 18.72, and no open-market selling.

One thing the record does not support is a volatility signature. At-the-money implied volatility from the option chains held essentially flat between 1 April and 9 April - roughly 56.7 per cent to 57.5 per cent - while the stock rose 16 per cent. The rally neither crushed the calls nor spiked them. The classification rests on structure and the dated report, not on volatility.

Observed after

What happened

February set the reference structure: a double top on 2 and 3 February 2026, at closes of 20.67 and 20.74 - the 52-week high in the daily price record. The stock then sold off to a low of 16.30 on 30 March, closing at 16.56, and reversed the next day, closing 8.7 per cent higher at 18.00. On 8 April it gapped up to close at 20.49, back at the February top, on 2.6 million shares. On 9 April it traded through the old high intraday - session high 21.55, close 21.33 - a new 52-week high.

The first-quarter report followed on 13 May, with an earnings surprise of +66.7 per cent and an 8 per cent gap. The advance ran through mid-June: on 9 June the stock traded a 54.15-66.65 range on 18.8 million shares - a blow-off reversal session - and on 18 June, the boundary of the study window, it closed at 64.90.

How a defined-risk expression behaved

The study takes a single long VSH July-2026 monthly 22.5 call. The chain held no June monthly at the entry date - VSH expiries ran April, May, July, October, December - and the May monthly would have expired on 15 May, two days after the dated report. July offered the nearest listed expiry beyond the catalyst. The entire risk of the structure is the premium paid.

At exit, with the stock at 64.52, intrinsic value stood at 42.02; the bid sat 1.42 below parity, which marks it as a real, dealable quotation. Together the two rows make the case on their own.

Interpretation

The discipline

What the episode teaches is narrow craft of repeated use. A V-reversal out of a panic low, followed within days by a break to a new 52-week high, in a cyclical component maker that the market had stopped watching, is a dated structural fact - not a story. The entry it supports is the close of the breakout session, at 21.52, roughly thirty per cent above the low. Nothing in the quote history above depends on having caught 16.30; the reconstruction enters on the day the evidence dated itself.

When the month you want is not listed, take the nearest expiry beyond the catalyst, not the one before it.

The second point: expiry selection. The dated report sat in mid-May; the May monthly expired on 15 May, two days after it, and would have sat outside the entire June advance. With no June monthly listed, the July contract - the nearest back month beyond the catalyst - offered the only expression that spanned both the event and the room after it. A single near-0.5-delta call sufficed; no spread overlay, and no attempt to time the low, appears anywhere in this history.

Evidence boundary

Evidence notes

This is a study of a move that had already played out. Because the move happened, the study takes the name - with no estimate of how often such setups resolve badly. The exit date is the boundary of the study window, not a signal. The honest reading is harsher: 9 June printed a blow-off reversal session (54.15-66.65 on 18.8 million shares), and a disciplined exit rule keyed to it would have left on 10 June with the stock near 54-57, meaningfully below the 18 June close used above. The boundary was the kinder exit, and that is a luck component.

Specific caveats. The volatility observations rest solely on call mid implied volatility from the chains. Open interest at entry stood at 167 contracts: a single lot would have dealt at the printed market, but any size beyond a handful of lots would have consumed a visible share of that liquidity - the underlying’s roughly $212 million of daily stock turnover softens, without removing, the point. The entry spread was 22 per cent of mid - wide, though flanked by consistent neighbouring strikes. The sector narrative around passive components and AI is not dated in the records used here, and so forms no part of the evidence. Finally, the chain's exit spot of 64.52 differs from the adjusted close of 64.90 by 0.6 per cent, a snapshot-timing artefact.
