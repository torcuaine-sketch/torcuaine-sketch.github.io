WOLF - historical reconstruction, 30 Mar to 24 Jul 2026

Wolfspeed (WOLF), 30 March to 24 July 2026. A reconstruction of a post-reorganisation reversal in a relisted semiconductor maker, assembled from dated exchange records and end-of-day option quotations.

Type
Reconstruction
Window
30 Mar - 24 Jul 2026
Instrument
WOLF Jun-2026 monthly 50 call
Fills basis
End-of-day chains, crossed

Over the window WOLF first rose 396.6 per cent on adjusted closes, from 14.80 on 30 March to 73.50 on 26 May, and then fell 68.6 per cent, to 23.09 by 24 July - both legs reproduced from adjusted closes, with no split artefacts. The studied trade runs from 6 May to 13 May. Two questions drive the note: what carried a date before the move, and how the market quoted one defined-risk option structure through it.

Observed before

What was knowable beforehand

The 5 May report predates the entry; its figures - the +13.8 per cent earnings surprise, the first since the reorganisation - stood public. The entry signal stood complete by the close of 6 May: a gap down, fully bought, to a new high, on record volume. Every price in that sentence is a dated exchange record.

The volatility record sharpened the entry. At-the-money one-month implied volatility fell from 173.7 on 4 May to 122.8 on 6 May: the event premium unloaded precisely as the trend confirmed itself, leaving calls cheap relative to a live trend, with the term structure nearly flat (second month at 110). The exit, too, ran on a date, not on discretion. On 13 May one-month implied volatility spiked to 223.4 with an eighty-point first-to-second-month inversion, alongside the rejection from the 73.74 intraday high - a blow-off signature, and the reconstruction's exit signal.

Observed after

What happened

The first leg ran a series of higher highs from 8 April - 20.6 to 43 - on rising call open interest, from 21.8 thousand to 88.5 thousand contracts. On 5 May the company reported: an earnings surprise of +13.8 per cent with revenue up 0.1 per cent - the first post-reorganisation beat. On 6 May the stock gapped down to 32.0, eleven per cent below the prior close, and buyers took it back entirely, to a new move high of 43.38. It closed at 43.08, up 17.6 per cent, on record volume of 14.8 million shares: a textbook earnings reversal to a new high.

The run continued to an intraday high of 73.74 on 13 May, which the stock rejected, closing at 62.60 - with the volatility signature described below. The second leg, after a double rejection at 80.82 and 80.70 on 22 and 26 May and a break on 27 May on record down-volume, took the stock to 23.09 by 24 July.

How a defined-risk expression behaved

The study takes a single long WOLF June-2026 monthly 50 call: delta 0.395, roughly eighteen per cent out of the money at a spot of 42.24, with 44 days to expiry. The July monthly was not yet listed on 6 May, so June offered the nearest reasonable expiry covering the signal horizon. Loss on the structure stops at the premium paid.

Both crossings run complete: the ask at entry, the bid at exit. At entry the spread measured 0.40 on a mid of 3.70, about eleven per cent, and the neighbouring strikes ran monotonic - the 45 call quoted 4.95/5.35, the 47.5 call 4.15/4.55 - consistent with a live rather than stale market. At exit the bid of 17.45 sat above the intrinsic value of 13.71, a quotation consistent with one-month implied volatility at 223. The volatility expansion after entry, in other words, worked for the long call rather than against it - the inverse of the usual post-earnings experience.

Interpretation

The discipline

The best-dated point to buy premium is a dated catalyst followed by a volatility crush on a confirmed breakout. On 6 May the event premium unloaded - 173.7 to 122.8 - exactly as the reversal on record volume confirmed the trend. The symmetric rule governs the exit: when one-month implied volatility spikes to 223 with an eighty-point term inversion, the rule closes the position without requiring a judgement about whether the move is finished.

The rule buys premium on the crush and leaves it on the spike.

The second leg carries the matching lesson for the short side. The decline read plainly - a double rejection at 80.82 and 80.70 on 22 and 26 May, then the 27 May break on record down-volume - but puts were already expensive, with implied volatility near 140. The July-17 50 put, quoted 6.10 and later 19.75 through a 69 per cent decline in the underlying, absorbed much of the move in premium. After a parabola with implied volatility above 140, the short expression belongs elsewhere than in naked puts.

Evidence boundary

Evidence notes

The page rebuilds a move that had already happened in full. The name is here for the move that occurred; how often comparable setups resolve badly goes unestimated. Specific caveats. The chain's entry spot of 42.24 sits about two per cent below the adjusted close of 43.08 - an end-of-day snapshot on a +17.6 per cent day with a 32.0-43.4 range; a real fill on such a day would likely have been worse than the print. The conservative exit on the 13 May volatility spike left the second half of the leg behind: an exit on the 26 May double-rejection close would have quoted 23.80 against the 3.90 entry, but that is closer to top-picking and is not claimed.

The 5 May announcement date stands reconstructed after the fact; the reaction may have occurred before the open on 6 May. In either case the figures stood public before the end-of-day entry on 6 May. News coverage of WOLF is thin, a post-Chapter-11 ticker relisted around October 2025, and the driver of the 13 May gap (a +24 per cent open) is undocumented; entry and exit do not depend on it. The volatility signatures come from the core at-the-money one- and two-month readings. Only one insider filing is used - the CEO, Feurle, on 1 May, a tax-withholding code - and it carries no signal.
