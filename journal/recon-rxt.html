RXT - historical reconstruction, 27 Mar to 17 Jun 2026

Rackspace Technology (RXT), 27 March to 17 June 2026. A reconstruction of a parabolic advance in a neglected small-cap running straight into a dated quarterly report, assembled from dated exchange records and end-of-day option quotations.

Type
Reconstruction
Window
27 Mar - 17 Jun 2026
Instrument
RXT Aug-2026 monthly 3 call
Fills basis
End-of-day chains, crossed

Over the window RXT rose 617.1 per cent on adjusted closes, from 1.05 to 7.53. The local price record matches the adjusted series one-to-one, with no split artefacts - the adjusted and unadjusted chain series are identical. The studied leg runs from 6 May to 15 May. This page examines the dated-in-advance elements of the move, plus the quoted path of one defined-risk option structure.

Observed before

What was knowable beforehand

The dated element: the catalyst. The first-quarter report of 7 May sat on the calendar, and the reconstruction places its entry at the close of 6 May, the day before. The structural element is the break with momentum behind it: a parabola on rising volume, running into a known report date, in a name the market had stopped watching at a dollar a share.

The print's direction became clear only after release. The actual first-quarter earnings surprise came in at −86.5 per cent, with revenue up 2.6 per cent; the rally ran on the revenue and AI-pivot narrative, not on the earnings number. Had the report gapped the stock down, most of the entry premium would have been lost. The reconstruction claims no foreknowledge of the figures - only of the date, the structure break, and the acceleration. Direction came from those, not from guessing the report, and that division of labour is the transferable part.

Observed after

What happened

The advance began from 1.05 in late March. On 10 April the stock jumped fifty per cent in a session; the catalyst for that jump is not identified by the dated record used here, which holds no news until June, so attribution there rests on price and volume alone. Into the first-quarter report, dated 7 May, the advance accelerated on rising volume and printed the period's largest volume day at 20.7 million shares - roughly three times average, and 2.8 to 3.4 times average across ten-to-twenty-day windows.

The report arrived on 7 May and the post-earnings run went parabolic. On 15 May came the first reversal day after the run: an open at 6.74 and a close at 5.82, twenty per cent below the prior close - a distribution session. The reconstruction's exit is that close. The advance continued afterwards, to 7.53 by mid-June, but that is outside the studied leg.

How a defined-risk expression behaved

The study takes a single long RXT August-2026 monthly 3 call - a back month, per the method's rule for a parabola into a dated event. August listed no 2.5 strike; the nearest real strike was 3, with the spot at 2.26. The structure cannot lose more than its premium.

The reconstruction pays the whole ask at entry and takes the whole bid at exit. At entry the spread measured 33 per cent of mid - wide, as small-cap options run - but open interest stood at 3,493 contracts, and a fill at the full ask is conservative yet real. The 108 days to expiry did the work the method asks of them. The contract went on at roughly 190 per cent implied volatility, absorbed the post-event volatility crush, and by the exit intrinsic value carried the quotation: a bid of 3.40 against a spot of 6.14, having been entered at 0.70 with the spot at 2.26. The exit-day chain spot of 6.14 differs from the adjusted close of 5.82 because the snapshot dates from intraday, around 15:45; the bid of 3.40 stands as printed.

Interpretation

The discipline

A small-cap parabola on rising volume, running into a known report date, is a back-month call and not a front-week one. The August contract with 108 days to expiry survived both the entry at 190 per cent implied volatility and the post-event crush, because intrinsic value took over the quotation; a front-week structure would have been at the mercy of the crush alone.

The dated event supplies the when; the structure break supplies the direction; the back month supplies the time.

The exit rule is mechanical: the first minus-twenty-per-cent reversal day after a parabolic post-earnings run. It required no guess about where the June top would form, and it sidestepped the 45 per cent drawdown the underlying suffered between 15 May and 20 May. Neither the entry nor the exit needed an opinion about the report itself.

Evidence boundary

Evidence notes

A completed move sits at the centre of this reconstruction. The move's occurrence selected the name; the page does not estimate how often comparable setups resolve badly. Specific caveats. The entry spread of 0.50/0.70 measured 33 per cent of mid; the fill at the full ask stays conservative but realistic given open interest of 3,493. The strike printed 122 contracts that day, so any order beyond a handful of lots would have consumed a visible share of the available liquidity - the study quotes prices, it does not claim capacity.

The direction of the print was not knowledge: the earnings surprise came in at −86.5 per cent and the rally ran on narrative; a downward gap would have consumed most of the 0.70 debit. The catalyst of the 10 April fifty-per-cent jump is not identified by the dated record used here (no news until June), so that session is attributed by price and volume only. If the release in fact came after the close on 6 May, the 6 May end-of-day quotations remain valid as of their date, as the snapshot precedes the reaction. A six-month implied-volatility reading of 317 per cent on 30 April looks like a single chain artefact, so the study excludes it. Finally, insider sales in February and March stood as a counter-signal that some disciplines would have treated as disqualifying; the reconstruction notes them and proceeds on structure alone.
