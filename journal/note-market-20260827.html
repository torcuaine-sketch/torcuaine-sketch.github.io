Market data note (multi-instrument), 27 August 2026

Market overview, 27 August 2026. A daily note from public exchange records and end-of-day option data, covering AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, TSLA.

The 27 August 2026 session was defined by a single catalyst with broad reach. Nvidia's earnings beat and forward revenue guidance - reported as reinforcing expectations of roughly 70% further revenue growth - pushed the semiconductor name sharply higher and lifted sentiment across the large-cap complex, with the S&P 500 up 0.66% on the day. All four names in the covered universe finished positive, a 4-for-4 same-sign session, but the uniformity of direction concealed meaningful differences in conviction and composition. Apple was the clear laggard at plus 0.36%, Microsoft and Tesla posted mid-range advances of 1.75% and 2.60% respectively, and Nvidia dominated at plus 8.74%. The session therefore reads less as a synchronized technology rally than as one company's event radiating outward with diminishing intensity across names that share index weight but not the underlying driver.

Nvidia's move carried the statistical signature of a genuine idiosyncratic event rather than beta participation. The stock closed at 227.98, precisely its 20-day high, on volume running 2.43 times the 20-day average - the only name in the group with elevated turnover. Its residual versus its peer basket was plus 8.29%, a 60-day z-score of 2.84, indicating the advance was almost entirely unexplained by basket movement. Options pricing told a quieter story: 30-day implied volatility of 35.99% sits at only the 24.2 percentile of its one-year range, suggesting the move, while large, did not breach what the options market had priced as plausible. A reported $12.9 billion acquisition of Hugging Face circulated during the session but remained unconfirmed, and the coverage of the item itself assigned it only moderate readthrough pending formal acknowledgment.

The two remaining technology names illustrate how a shared headline can mask opposite internal dynamics. Microsoft rose 1.75% to 505.06, finishing just 0.2% below its 20-day high, yet its residual versus its software basket was negative 5.61%, a 60-day z-score of negative 1.66 - the stock advanced on the tape while lagging the basket it is measured against. Apple, by contrast, showed neither index sympathy nor idiosyncratic thrust: its 0.36% advance came on volume at 0.69 times the 20-day average, the lightest relative turnover in the group, with the close at 314.58 still 0.71% below its recent high. Its basket residual was a negligible z-score of 0.12, and its 30-day drift z-score of negative 1.63 is the weakest in the universe, marking it as the name where the session's enthusiasm simply did not register.

Tesla occupied a middle position that complicates any clean technology-versus-everything-else framing. The consumer discretionary name advanced 2.60% to 354.81 with a basket residual z-score of 0.87 - a mild idiosyncratic positive on volume slightly below average at 0.92 times the 20-day norm - yet it remains 2.22% below its 20-day high, the deepest discount to recent highs in the group. Its implied volatility of 40.14% is the highest in the universe but ranks at only the 14.4 percentile of its own one-year history. Taken together, the drift hierarchy - Apple weakest, Tesla next, Nvidia and Microsoft strongest - suggests the session amplified existing trends rather than reversing them: the catalyst-rich leader extended, the two quiet names drifted sideways on thin participation, and the one non-technology name borrowed strength from the tape without generating a catalyst of its own.
