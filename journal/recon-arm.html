ARM - historical reconstruction, 30 Mar to 18 Jun 2026

ARM, 30 March to 18 June 2026. A reconstruction of a structural break in a megacap semiconductor name, assembled from dated exchange records and end-of-day option quotations.

Type
Reconstruction
Window
30 Mar - 18 Jun 2026
Instrument
ARM Jun-2026 monthly 220 call
Fills basis
End-of-day chains, crossed

Over the window ARM rose 220.9 per cent on adjusted closes, from 136.96 to 439.46, with no split artefacts in the series. This note examines which parts of that move were datable in advance and how the market quoted one defined-risk option structure through it.

Observed before

What was knowable beforehand

Every element of the entry case carries a date before 23 April.

The reference level sat in public view: a 52-week closing high of 178.62, recorded 27 October 2025. The 25 March ignition gap carried a date but deliberately did not count as a signal - it broke no level and had no dated news explanation. The signal was the 22 April close at 196.57, a 12 per cent day on 13.9 million shares through the level, after the two closes at 175.10 and 175.49 just beneath it.

Two further dated facts sat alongside the break. First, a peer catalyst carried the same date: Intel’s first-quarter results, with an explicit read-through to ARM and AMD - the news record of 23 April, classified bull at materiality 3, headlined “AMD, Arm Stocks Ride Intel’s Coattails After Blowout Q1 Report”. Second, cadence dated ARM’s own report: the prior one fell on 4 February, placing the next in early May - it in fact arrived on 6 May - inside the life of a back-month option.

Observed after

What happened

The window opens at a closing low of 136.96 on 30 March. Five days earlier, on 25 March, the stock had gapped 16.4 per cent, from 134.96 to 157.07, on 29.6 million shares - roughly six times median volume - without breaking any prior level and without an attributable dated news item. The advance then stalled: closes of 175.10 and 175.49 sat just beneath the 52-week closing high of 178.62, set on 27 October 2025.

On 22 April the level went. The stock closed at 196.57, up 12 per cent on the day on 13.9 million shares, clearing the October high. Its own quarterly report followed on 6 May with an EPS beat of 3.4 per cent. On 1 June, after doubling in eight sessions, the stock gapped a further 15.7 per cent. Between 5 and 10 June it retraced from 411 to 307, then recovered; the window closes on 18 June at 439.46.

How a defined-risk expression behaved

The study takes a single long ARM June-2026 monthly 220 call. The June monthly expired on Thursday 18 June because of the Juneteenth holiday. The expiry choice spanned the report expected in early May; the strike put the option near 0.47 delta at entry. At worst the structure loses its premium - no more.

Entry pays the whole ask; exit takes the whole bid. The adjusted record puts the two sessions’ closes at 204.61 and 439.46. At exit, on expiry day, the bid sat $1.95 below intrinsic value, which marks it as a real, tradeable quotation rather than a printed courtesy. The path between the two rows was not smooth: during the 5-10 June retracement of the underlying from 411 to 307, the call’s quoted value fell sharply before recovering into expiry.

Interpretation

The discipline

The reconstruction illustrates three habits, none of them exotic. First, in a megacap semiconductor, a 52-week-high break on volume, a same-dated peer catalyst and a dated report inside the option’s life argue for a back-month call near 0.45 delta that spans the event - not short-dated weeklies that expire before the catalyst can matter.

The first gap had no level and no explanation. The second move had both. Patience between the two makes the entire method.

Second, the 25 March ignition gap - dramatic, high-volume, unexplained - was not the entry. It broke nothing and explained nothing. The entry waited four weeks for a structural break with a dated cause attached.

Third, and most demanding: the main work in this history was not the entry but the holding. The 5-10 June retracement from 411 to 307 cut deep enough that, without a rule fixed in advance - hold to expiry unless structure breaks lower - the natural response would have been to abandon the position in the middle of the move. The rule has to exist before the drawdown does.

Evidence boundary

Evidence notes

The reconstruction starts from a move known in advance to have occurred. The move's occurrence selected this name; nothing here estimates how often comparable setups resolve badly, and the survival of the 5-10 June retracement is validated only in retrospect. That is a luck component, and it counts as one.

Specific caveats. The cause of the 25 March gap was never established from dated records; news coverage of ARM is sparse before April. The volatility signature could not be checked, so the classification rests on structure alone. The end-of-day chain spot differed from the adjusted close by roughly 0.8 per cent at entry (202.99 against 204.61), a snapshot-timing artefact. The expiry-day exit at a bid below intrinsic is conservative; a plausible alternative discipline - exiting into the 1 June blowoff gap, a 15.7 per cent gap after a doubling in eight sessions - would have occurred with the underlying near 408, well below the eventual close. Finally, the window opens at the 136.96 low but the signal entry is later and higher, at 202.99; the reconstruction measures a rule-dated segment, not low-to-high. Entry spread was 6.4 per cent of mid - liquid by the standards of the chain.
