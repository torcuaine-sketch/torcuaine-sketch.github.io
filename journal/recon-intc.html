INTC - historical reconstruction, 30 Mar to 22 Jun 2026

Intel Corporation (INTC), 30 March to 22 June 2026. A reconstruction of a megacap turnaround that announced itself through structure before it announced itself through earnings, assembled from dated exchange records and end-of-day option quotations.

Type
Reconstruction
Window
30 Mar - 22 Jun 2026
Instrument
INTC Jul-2026 monthly 55 call
Fills basis
End-of-day chains, crossed

Over the window INTC rose 242.1 per cent on adjusted closes, from 41.19 to 140.94. What carried a date before the move, and how the market quoted one defined-risk option structure through it, make up this note.

Observed before

What was knowable beforehand

Every element of the entry case carries a date on or before 6 April 2026, the session the reconstruction uses as its entry point. The report itself carried a date: the quarterly cadence ran 23 October 2025, then 22 January 2026, placing the next report near 23 April, and the earnings-events record confirms an announce date of 23 April 2026.

The surprise trend sat in the record. The 22 January 2026 report had shown an earnings surprise of +84.4 per cent, and the 23 October 2025 report one of +1,191 per cent - a series of outsized beats, not a single print. The structural break preceded the entry by two sessions: the 2 April close at 50.38, above both the intraday high of 49.17 (16 March) and the closing high of 47.98, on 117 million shares. The 4 April buyback headline completed the tape the entry rested on. The entry is after the break and the headline, and before the catalyst.

What the catalyst then delivered is a matter of record, not of foresight: an earnings surprise of +1,428 per cent, revenue up 9.3 per cent, and the 23 per cent gap of 24 April.

Observed after

What happened

Through March the stock held a defined range: an intraday high of 49.17 on 16 March and a closing high of 47.98. On 2 April it closed at 50.38, through the entire March range, on 117 million shares. On 4 April an aggregator carried a materiality-6 headline classed as a buyback announcement: “Massive News for Intel Stock Investors.” The reconstruction enters on the next session, 6 April, at an adjusted close of 50.78.

The first-quarter report arrived on 23 April: an earnings surprise of +1,428 per cent and revenue up 9.3 per cent. The next session, 24 April, the stock gapped 23 per cent - an open of 82.20 against a prior close of 66.78, on 281 million shares. The advance did not end with the gap. It ran for a further two months, through a blow-off session on 18 June (233 million shares, +10.6 per cent), to a close of 140.94 on 22 June, the boundary of the study window.

How a defined-risk expression behaved

The study takes a single long INTC July-2026 monthly 55 call - the back month through the 23 April report. The June monthly expired on 18 June, before the end of the window, so July, the listed expiry, spanned both the catalyst and the runway after it. The premium paid marks the worst case; beyond it there is nothing to lose.

Interpretation

The discipline

The episode illustrates the treatment of a megacap turnaround: a series of very large earnings surprises, a break of the multi-week base before a dated report, and an entry taken after the break rather than at the low. The expression that fits that evidence is a back-month call, not a weekly. With 103 days to expiry at entry, the structure carried through the event without a theta or volatility-crush death, and it remained alive for the entire post-report trend.

The report gap supplied the ignition, not the move. Do not exit the surprise leader on gap day while the trend structure is intact.

The second point: holding. The 23 per cent gap of 24 April only started it: the stock added roughly another 70 per cent over the following two months. A rule that banks the gap-day move in a name leading its cohort on earnings surprise abandons exactly the segment of the move the structure set out to span. The exit rule belongs to trend structure, not to the excitement of the event.

Evidence boundary

Evidence notes

This reconstruction works backward from a move that had already occurred. The move brought the name into the series; the odds of comparable setups resolving badly are not assessed here. The exit date bounds the study window and, in fact, landed close to the local top - by 24 July the stock had fallen to 92.32. That is hindsight-flavoured, and the note says so plainly. A systematic exit keyed to the 18 June blow-off volume (233 million shares, +10.6 per cent) would have left slightly earlier, with the 55 call bid near 79 at a spot near 134 - a similar, somewhat smaller figure.

Specific caveats. The volatility signature could not be verified, so the classification rests on the catalyst and the structure alone. The forward-knowability of the 23 April date rests on reporting cadence; the exact press-announcement date was not verified in advance. The 4 April buyback headline is an aggregator item (materiality 6) whose primary source was not checked. Finally, the chain exit spot of 140.26 differs from the adjusted close of 140.94 - an intraday snapshot artefact; the fill took the recorded bid - the conservative reading.

Unknown and limitations

Limitations

This is a reconstruction of a move already known to have happened. The name was selected because the move occurred; nothing here estimates how often comparable setups resolve badly. The volatility signature could not be verified: the historical chain dailies endpoint for INTC returned only price fields, with no 30-day implied volatility, so the classification rests on the catalyst and the structure alone. The event-calendar table holds no INTC row, so the forward-knowability of the 23 April date rests on reporting cadence; the exact press-announcement date was not verified point-in-time.
