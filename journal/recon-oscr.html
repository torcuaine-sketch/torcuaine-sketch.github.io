OSCR - historical reconstruction, 30 Mar to 2 Jul 2026

OSCR, 30 March to 2 July 2026. A reconstruction of a washed-out stock re-rated by a dated quarterly report, assembled from dated exchange records and end-of-day option quotations.

Type
Reconstruction
Window
30 Mar - 2 Jul 2026
Instrument
OSCR Jul-2026 monthly 23 call
Fills basis
End-of-day chains, crossed

Over the window OSCR rose 196.6 per cent on adjusted closes, from 10.85 to 32.18. The reconstruction enters roughly ninety per cent above the low, on the morning after the dated catalyst, and asks why that still came early enough.

Observed before

What was knowable beforehand

Every element of the entry case carries a date on or before 7 May 2026, the session the reconstruction uses as its entry point. The February failure and the washed-out tape sat in the record. So was a dated insider fact: a Form 4 filed 7 April showed the chief executive, Mark Bertolini, exercising 5.73 million options on 3 and 6 April with transactions at 11.92, his holding rising from 4.46 to 9.2 million shares - accumulation near the lows. The volatility structure of 5 May - the term inversion, the 16.2 per cent implied move - dated the event and priced it. And the report itself of 6 May was public before the entry: the +71.1 per cent earnings surprise, the new multi-month high on triple volume, and the collapse of the front-month implied volatility from 104.9 to 63.9.

Two qualifications belong in the record rather than the footnotes. The beat came on earnings only: the revenue surprise ran −6.5 per cent, so the thesis rested on margin, not top line. And the first leg of the advance - the 65 per cent between 31 March and 5 May, including the newsless gap of 8 April - had no dated signal behind it beyond the Form 4 of 7 April. The reconstruction honestly skips that leg and enters only on 7 May, after the catalyst, at a spot of 20.63 - roughly ninety per cent above the window low.

Observed after

What happened

The setup: a washed-out tape. On 10 February 2026 the fourth-quarter report missed - an earnings surprise of −34.6 per cent - and by 30 March the stock had sunk to 10.85. A first leg followed: between 31 March and 5 May the stock rose about 65 per cent, including a gap of +10.6 per cent on 8 April for which no dated news item is identified. On the eve of the next report, 5 May, the option cores showed the market holding a dated event: the term structure sat inverted, with one-month at-the-money implied volatility at 104.9 against 72.0 in the second month, an implied move of 16.2 per cent, and zero days to the next earnings date.

The report came on 6 May: an earnings surprise of +71.1 per cent and a gap-up close at 19.84 - a new multi-month high, through the April consolidation whose 1 May high was 18.66 - on 18.9 million shares, roughly three times normal volume. The front-month implied volatility collapsed from 104.9 to 63.9. The drift ran for eight weeks, through a drawdown stretch between 20 May and 3 June, to a thrust of +11.9 per cent on 1 July out of a three-week range; on 2 July, the boundary of the study window, the stock closed at 32.18. By 24 July it had pulled back to 28.

How a defined-risk expression behaved

The study takes a single long OSCR July-2026 monthly 23 call - slightly out of the money, seventy-two days to expiry, strike and expiry actually listed in the recorded chain. The downside of the structure stops at the premium paid.

At exit the bid of 8.55 sat $0.54 below the intrinsic value of 9.09 (the 32.09 spot less the 23 strike): the full crossing is conservative, and a resting limit order or an exercise would have dealt better. The comparison of the two rows stands without further comment.

Interpretation

The discipline

The episode illustrates the least heroic entry in the repertoire: the morning after. Nothing in the quote history above depends on having caught 10.85. The entry sits on the far side of a dated confirmation - a large earnings beat printed at a fresh multi-month high on triple volume - and the price of that confirmation, roughly ninety per cent above the low, is simply paid.

The volatility crush hands the option back cheap exactly when the drift statistics are at their best.

The second point is why the morning after, rather than the morning of. On the eve of the report the front-month implied volatility stood at 104.9 with an implied move of 16.2 per cent; by the entry session it had collapsed to 63.9. Buying the two-month slightly out-of-the-money call the day after the report buys the drift without buying the event premium. The third point concerns the insider fact. The exercise-and-hold near the lows is confirming, not triggering: its function is to justify holding through noise - specifically the drawdown stretch of 20 May to 3 June - not to license an entry before the catalyst exists.

Evidence boundary

Evidence notes

This study reconstructs a move whose outcome was already known. The move's occurrence put this name on the page; nothing here estimates how often comparable setups resolve the other way. The beat was earnings-only, against a revenue surprise of −6.5 per cent: the thesis rested on margin, and a margin-only beat is a weaker instrument than the headline suggests. The exit bid of 8.55 sat $0.54 below intrinsic value, so the crossing convention understates what a patient exit would have dealt.

Further caveats. The first leg of the advance - 65 per cent between 31 March and 5 May, including the +10.6 per cent gap of 8 April, for which no dated news item is identified - was not covered by any dated signal beyond the Form 4 of 7 April, and the reconstruction enters only afterwards; an earlier entry would have been hindsight in a costume. The exit date is the boundary of the study window: the sell-into-strength rationale - the +11.9 per cent thrust of 1 July out of a three-week range, with sixteen days to expiry - is real, but the choice of 2 July is partly hindsight, and the stock's pullback to 28 by 24 July means a later exit would have surrendered part of the move. The chain spots of 20.63 and 32.09 differ slightly from the adjusted closes of 20.87 and 32.18, a snapshot effect. Guidance history is unrecorded for this name, and the Form 4 purchase-and-surrender codes of 6 April - one million shares bought and one million surrendered at 11.92 - are exercise mechanics, interpreted here as exercise-and-hold for a net 4.7 million shares.
