CRDO - historical reconstruction, 30 Mar to 22 Jun 2026

CRDO rose 244.5 per cent between 30 March and 22 June 2026, from an adjusted close of 87.81 to 302.52. This note reconstructs what was datable before 13 April, and how one defined-risk option structure was quoted through the move.

Every claim below is tied to a dated session and taken from exchange records - adjusted exchange prices and end-of-day option quotations as recorded - and the calculations were rerun against the dated records. Instructiveness, not representativeness, is why the episode appears here.

Observed before

What was knowable beforehand

The reconstruction dates its entry to the close of 13 April 2026. Four facts sat on the record by then.

First, the 2 March report: an EPS surprise of +17.3 per cent, a fundamental beat. Second, the character of the decline that followed it - roughly 114 down to 86.49 with no company-specific negative on record - which read as sector beta rather than a thesis about the company. Third, the dated level: the double rejection of 123-126 on 16-17 March, with the 125.70 high and the 104.06 close on 15.6 million shares. Fourth, the recovery itself: the 10.8 per cent session of 10 April on 8.6 million shares, and then the 13 April close at 134.36 on twice average volume, taking out the whole March range and the rejection level in one session.

The signal was not that the shares rose. It was that a dated level, rejected twice on heavy volume, was taken out on twice average volume while the fundamental record showed a beat.

Two things stayed deliberately outside the evidence because they came later: the 15 per cent gap of 14 April, and everything around the 1 June report. Yet the 1 June earnings date itself was in the events calendar before entry, and it serves exactly one purpose below - the choice of expiry.

Observed after

What happened

On 2 March 2026 the company reported earnings with an EPS surprise of +17.3 per cent. The shares fell anyway: over the following four weeks the tape carried them from roughly 114 to 86.49, with no negative company-specific event in the record over that stretch. The decline ran sector-wide, and the stock closed the quarter, on 30 March, at 87.81.

On the way down, the market left a mark. On 16 and 17 March the shares twice tested and twice rejected the 123-126 zone; on 17 March the session printed a high of 125.70 and closed at 104.06 on 15.6 million shares - a large intraday rejection at a level that could thereafter be dated.

The recovery began on 10 April with a gain of 10.8 per cent on 8.6 million shares. On 13 April the stock closed at 134.36 on 10.4 million shares - roughly twice average volume - above the entire March range and above the 125.70 rejection level, a new recovery high. The following session gapped up 15 per cent. Earnings on 1 June produced a muddled two-day reaction, 226 to 214, after which the advance resumed. The window closed on 22 June at 302.52, on a gap associated with an Evercore price target of 325. The next session the shares fell 10 per cent, to 272.

How a defined-risk expression behaved

The study takes a single long call in a back-month expiry placed beyond the nearest dated event: the 21 August 2026 monthly 150 call, a listed strike, carrying a delta of 0.53 at entry. The expiry sits behind the 1 June earnings date so that the position does not have to be right about the event's timing, only about the direction of the repair. All fills cross the end-of-day quoted spread - buying at the ask, selling at the bid - the conservative convention throughout these notes.

The path between those two quotations was not smooth, and the structure's construction carried it through. The early, structural entry built an in-the-money buffer before the first serious test: the pullback from 172 to 150 between 15 and 18 May, a decline of 13 per cent, and then the two-day earnings reaction from 226 to 214. A cheap front-month call bought on the same signal would have crossed both stretches near failure; the back-month structure was never close to it.

Interpretation

The discipline

The general shape deserves plain statement. When the leader of a theme falls 31 per cent in a sector correction while its own most recent report is a beat, the decline carries no information about the company. When that same stock then breaks a dated, twice-rejected level on twice average volume after a V-shaped repair, the break is an entry condition, not a chase: the level did the dating, and the volume did the confirming.

The expression follows from the calendar. With a dated event ahead - here, 1 June - the expiry belongs behind the event, and the strike belongs near a delta of 0.5. The path above makes the point of that construction plain: it buys tolerance for noise. The position had one task: holding through shakeouts while the structure of higher lows stayed intact - and that structure never broke. A front-month expression asks the same thesis to also be right about timing, which is a different and harder claim.

Evidence boundary

Evidence notes

The volatility record could not be verified. No implied-volatility history is used here, so the classification of this episode rests on price and volume alone. Whatever the options market said about the March decline stays out of this note.

The catalyst behind the 14 April gap of 15 per cent is not identified; news coverage of the name before May 2026 is sparse. So the April leg reads as structure rather than as an identified event. The entry did not depend on that gap, but the attribution is an inference, not a documented fact.

The exit date marks the end of the study window, not a mechanical signal. It landed on the 22 June gap associated with the Evercore 325 target - a blowoff session - and the shares fell 10 per cent the next day. The placement of that endpoint flatters the reconstruction, and the flattery is luck, not method. An alternative expression in the 18 June front-month existed and would have forced an exit four days before the window's end; it is noted here only to record that the back-month choice was not the only listed path.

Small snapshot differences exist between sources - chain spot 134.69 against an adjusted close of 134.36 at entry, roughly 0.2 per cent - and do not affect the sequence. The entry quotation ran deep and liquid, with a spread of 6 per cent of mid, so stale-quote risk stays low. Finally, the move's occurrence selected the episode for study. Selection of that kind flatters any method, and this page neither makes nor implies a frequency claim - how often such setups appear, or how often they resolve this way.
