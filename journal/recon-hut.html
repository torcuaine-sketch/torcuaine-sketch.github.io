HUT - historical reconstruction, 30 Mar to 2 Jun 2026

Hut 8 (HUT), 30 March to 2 June 2026. A reconstruction of a gap above a seven-week base in the bitcoin-mining complex, assembled from dated exchange records and end-of-day option quotations.

Type
Reconstruction
Window
30 Mar - 2 Jun 2026
Instrument
HUT Jun-2026 monthly 70 call
Fills basis
End-of-day chains, crossed

Over the window HUT rose 211.4 per cent on adjusted closes, from 42.72 to 133.02. The note separates the datable parts of the move from the rest and follows the quotations of one defined-risk option structure.

Observed before

What was knowable beforehand

Every element of the entry case is dated on or before 9 April 2026, the session the reconstruction uses as its entry point. The seven-week base and its top at 56.87 stood public from 25 March. The 8 April gap above the whole base - open 57.09, close 61.43, up 16.7 per cent on 11.1 million shares, about 2.5 times the average - sat in the record before entry. Crucially, the move was sectoral rather than idiosyncratic: on the same day MARA rose 6.0 per cent, RIOT 13.5 per cent and CLSK 9.0 per cent, with the bitcoin-mining complex in bid across the board - breadth confirmation, dated the day before entry.

A dated catalyst sat further along the track. The earnings record showed reports on 4 November 2025 and 25 February 2026, and the quarterly cadence placed the next report in early May; it in fact arrived on 6 May, with an earnings surprise of +57.1 per cent and a gap of +35.4 per cent. One thing the record does not support is a volatility signature: 30-day implied volatility was essentially flat at roughly 96 to 100 per cent through the entire breakout - neither a spike nor a crush - so the classification rests honestly on the structural break alone.

Observed after

What happened

Through March 2026 the stock built a seven-week base, with a top at 56.87 - the high of 25 March - and closes ranging between 42.72 and 55.62. On 8 April it gapped above the entire base: the open at 57.09 already sat above the March high, and the stock closed at 61.43, up 16.7 per cent, on 11.1 million shares - roughly two and a half times the average of about 4.2 million.

The first-quarter report arrived on 6 May, with an earnings surprise of +57.1 per cent and a gap of +35.4 per cent - the principal driver of the rest of the move. The advance ended in parabolic exhaustion: six consecutive up sessions from the 19 May low of 86.82 (+41 per cent), and on 2 June - the boundary of the study window - a new high at 140.80 but the first red candle of the run, closing at 133.02 against an open of 134.19.

How a defined-risk expression behaved

The study takes a single long HUT June-2026 monthly 70 call - at-the-money, delta 0.50 at entry, a back month chosen to run through the dated 6 May report. Whatever happens, the loss ends at the premium paid.

Going in costs the full ask; going out returns the full bid. At exit, with the chain spot at 131.72, intrinsic value stood at 61.72; the exit took the bid of 60.30, 1.42 below parity - a conservative full crossing, where exercise would have yielded slightly more. The two rows speak for themselves when placed together.

Interpretation

The discipline

The episode illustrates a narrow, repeatedly useful piece of craft. A breakout from a multi-week base on two-and-a-half times average volume, in a high-beta complex, is meaningfully stronger when the whole sector moves together - and when it does, the expression belongs in the day's leader. On 8 April that meant HUT at +16.7 per cent against peers at +6.0 to +13.5 per cent. The entry the reconstruction uses is the session after the break, at 63.12 - honestly after the signal, not inside the base.

A flat implied-volatility surface through a breakout amounts to no tax on long premium. That is no invitation to confuse quiet with safe.

The second point: the term of the contract relative to the dated event. With the report about four weeks ahead, the June monthly carried the structure through it: the 6 May earnings gap of +35.4 per cent supplied the principal part of the later move, and holding the back month removed any need to roll across the event. The flat implied volatility of roughly 96 to 100 per cent through the breakout meant the market was not marking the long premium up into the move - the structure paid for time, not for panic.

Evidence boundary

Evidence notes

The move under study had already run its course before this reconstruction. This name is present because its move occurred, and the page leaves the failure rate of comparable setups unmeasured. The exit date is keyed to parabolic exhaustion after the fact - six consecutive up sessions and the first red candle - and a rule phrased that loosely will both leave early and stay late across a larger sample.

Specific caveats. The April breakout catalyst is not identified from dated records before 19 May, so the entry is justified by price, volume and sector breadth, not by a company-specific event. The exact 6 May report date emerged only later; early May is an inference from the cadence of past reports, though the entry does not depend on it. The exit at the bid of 60.30 sat 1.42 below intrinsic value of 61.72, a conservative crossing. The chain exit spot of 131.72 differs from the adjusted close of 133.02, a snapshot-timing artefact. Finally, the structure captures the move from 63 to 133 - roughly +110 per cent in the underlying after the signal - not the full +211.4 per cent from the window low; the entry is honestly after the breakout, and the earlier part of the move belongs to whoever was positioned inside the base.
