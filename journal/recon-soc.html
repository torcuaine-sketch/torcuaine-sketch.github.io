SOC - historical reconstruction, 27 Mar to 30 Jun 2026

SOC, 27 March to 30 June 2026. A reconstruction of a decline in a pre-revenue story stock, assembled from dated exchange records and end-of-day option quotations.

Type
Reconstruction
Window
27 Mar - 30 Jun 2026
Instrument
SOC Jul-2026 monthly 8 put
Fills basis
End-of-day chains, crossed

Over the window SOC fell 83.3 per cent on adjusted closes, from 18.45 to 3.08. This note examines which parts of that decline carried a date before it happened, and how the market quoted one defined-risk option structure through it.

Observed before

What was knowable beforehand

Every element of the entry case carries a date on or before 22 June 2026, the session the reconstruction uses as its entry point. The broken parabola sat in the record from the double rejection of 27 and 30 March (high 19.21, close 17.84) and the lower lows that followed. The earnings record - the −114 per cent earnings surprise and −92 per cent revenue surprise of 6 May, against an unbroken run of revenue misses - stood public. The distress chain itself consisted of four dated events inside three weeks: the emergency call announced 29 May and held 1 June, the second call announced 11 June and held 12 June, the senior secured term loan of 16 June, and the term-loan amendment with a limited waiver of 22 June. A waiver under a purchase and sale agreement is covenant stress in writing; at a company with no revenue, it makes the risk of a forced equity raise explicit.

The volatility tape corroborated rather than signalled. The iv30 jump from 1.16 to 1.53 on the call announcement of 29 May, and the term inversion on 12 June, amounted to the market pricing dated bad news in real time. The exact 30 June offer date emerged later. The chain telegraphed the direction and the mechanism - a forced raise; the exact morning it did not, and the reconstruction does not pretend otherwise.

Observed after

What happened

The reference structure: a parabola - between 2 March and 27 March 2026 the stock rose 115 per cent, from 8.60 to 18.45. The break came at the top: a double rejection on 27 and 30 March - session high 19.21, close 17.84 - followed by a series of lower lows through April, May and June. On 6 May the company reported with an earnings surprise of −114 per cent and a revenue surprise of −92 per cent; every quarter in the record shows a revenue miss between −61 and −98 per cent. The company was, on the tape, effectively pre-revenue.

Then the distress events arrived in sequence. On 29 May the company announced an emergency conference call for 1 June; the 30-day implied volatility jumped from 1.16 to 1.53 the same day, and the day of the call closed −11.2 per cent on roughly two and a half times normal volume, in a 16.08-12.48 range. On 11 June the company announced a second emergency call for 12 June; that session printed new multi-month lows at 10.88, below the April low of 12.51, with the chain term structure inverted (rSlp30 −2.51). On 16 June came the headline “Commencement of New Senior Secured Term Loan” - a pre-revenue company taking secured debt. On 22 June, at 08:00 before the open: “Term Loan Amendment and Limited Waiver Under the PSA.” On 30 June, at 08:08 before the open, the company announced an offering of shares and convertibles. The stock fell 56 per cent that day on 97 million shares.

How a defined-risk expression behaved

The study takes a single long SOC July-2026 monthly 8 put, bought after the waiver headline of 22 June. The premium paid caps the loss on the structure.

Fills cross the entire quoted spread - ask at entry, bid at exit. The exit chain on a −56 per cent day ran wide in places - the neighbouring 7.5 strike quoted 3.70 / 4.45 - but the 8 strike held a comparatively tight 4.65 / 4.95, roughly six per cent of mid. With the stock at 3.21, the intrinsic value of the 8 put was 4.79; the bid at 4.65 sat 0.14 below parity, which marks it as a real, dealable quotation rather than a stale print. Entry came with implied volatility already above 130 per cent: the option did not come cheap, and the reconstruction does not claim it did.

Interpretation

The discipline

The episode illustrates a narrow, repeatedly useful piece of craft, run here in the declining direction. A distress chain at a pre-revenue story stock after a broken parabola - emergency call, second emergency call, secured term loan, limited waiver - telegraphs a forced dilution. The method never needs the offer's date. A put with roughly a month of life, bought after the waiver headline, gives the mechanism time to operate; the position goes on against new lows and expensive volatility because a forced raise breaks the price faster than theta consumes the premium.

With an undated catalyst, the expiry is the thesis: three to four weeks, never the coming Friday.

The second point: time with margin. The 26 June weekly expired two trading days before the collapse of 30 June and would have expired worthless. The July monthly - 26 days to expiry at entry - stood as the nearest contract whose life comfortably spanned an event with no set date. The alternative weekly of 2 July, the 7.5 put offered at 0.24, was quoted far higher at exit; selecting it bets on the exact timing of an undated event, and the method rejects it.

Evidence boundary

Evidence notes

The move reconstructed here was already complete when the work started. The move made the selection; no figure here says how often comparable setups resolve the other way. The end-of-day chain spot on 30 June is 3.21 against an adjusted adjusted close of 3.08 - a snapshot divergence of roughly four per cent; the fills are taken from real chain quotations and are unaffected by it. Entry fills are end-of-day quotations on the news day itself: the headline arrived at 08:00 and the fill comes from the evening chain, so intraday prices could have dealt better or worse.

Further caveats. The greater part of the −83 per cent window preceded the entry; the study honestly takes only the distress leg from 9.25 to 3.21, and an entry from the 27 March top would have required backfitting a signal that did not exist. Option open interest and volume were not verified: at roughly $47 million of daily stock turnover the capacity of the structure is limited to tens or hundreds of contracts, not thousands. The note records the wide quotes elsewhere in the exit chain above. None of these caveats is decorative.
