NVTS - historical reconstruction, 30 Mar to 24 Jul 2026

Navitas Semiconductor (NVTS), 30 March to 24 July 2026. A reconstruction of a parabolic advance and its unwinding in a power-semiconductor maker, assembled from dated exchange records and end-of-day option quotations.

Type
Reconstruction
Window
30 Mar - 24 Jul 2026
Instrument
NVTS Jul-2026 monthly 25 put
Fills basis
End-of-day chains, crossed

Over the window NVTS first rose 306.0 per cent on adjusted closes, from 7.83 on 30 March to 31.79 on 26 May, and then fell 65.6 per cent, to 10.92 by 24 July. The studied leg runs from 4 June to 16 July: a decline of 61.9 per cent, from 30.67 to 11.755. Which parts of the reversal were datable beforehand, and how the market quoted one defined-risk option structure through it, is the note's subject.

Observed before

What was knowable beforehand

Every element of the short case carries a date on or before the close of 3 June 2026; the reconstruction enters on 4 June, strictly after the signal had finished forming. The parabola itself, the 26 May blow-off bar, the 27 May first rejection and the 3 June retest-rejection on record volume all sit in dated exchange records. A short taken on the first red day, 27 May, would have met a twenty per cent intraday squeeze on 3 June, to 34.17. The dated trigger is the retest that failed.

The volatility record corroborates, as of its dates. Thirty-day implied volatility rose together with the price, from 111 per cent on 15 April to a peak of 156 per cent on 26 May, and on 3 June the term-structure contango reading was −8.82 - the deepest inversion of the series, with ten-day implied volatility at 170 per cent. That is the signature of a speculative blow-off, not of hedging demand.

Weaker supporting evidence sat in the insider tape: the founder, Singh Ranbir, sold 389,000 shares ($3.7 million) on 26 February, and the CFO, Glickman, sold $1.06 million on 17 March - Form 4 filings dated long before the peak. The up-leg had its own dated entry - a 16 April breakout above the March high of 11.63 on 50.7 million shares, closing 12.37, worth 157 per cent to the peak - but the dated record used here identifies no catalyst under 16 April; dated news in this record starts on 7 May. That leg's classification is honestly weaker. The short leg on the reversal is the cleaner structure break.

Observed after

What happened

The advance ran parabolic: 306 per cent close-to-close over 38 sessions, from 7.83 on 30 March to 31.79 on 26 May, of which 64 per cent came in the final five sessions alone - 19.43 on 19 May to 31.79 on 26 May - all dated exchange bars. 26 May was the blow-off: session high 33.82, close 31.79. 27 May brought the first rejection: open 31.81, close 28.88, down 9.2 per cent.

The key day: 3 June. The stock gapped up to retest 34.17 - barely one per cent above the 26 May high - on the window's record volume of 112.3 million shares, and closed at 30.84, near its open of 30.645: a second rejection, distribution rather than accumulation. It did so on a day of bullish news (“Why NVTS Stock Is Skyrocketing Today”, dated 3 June in the news record): a gap on good news that was not held. The decline then ran. The studied leg from 4 June to 16 July covers 30.67 to 11.755, and by 24 July the stock closed at 10.92.

How a defined-risk expression behaved

The study takes a single long NVTS July-2026 monthly 25 put. The chain listed no August monthly at the entry date - after the July expiry it went directly to 18 September - so July, at 44 days to expiry, was the nearest listed expiry beyond the signal horizon. Its premium is the full extent of what the structure can lose.

Interpretation

The discipline

The episode yields three forward rules. First, do not short a parabola's first red day. The dated trigger is the retest-rejection: a gap-up to the old high that is not held despite bullish news, on record volume, with the deepest term-structure inversion of the series. Second, enter the day after the signal candle completes. That convention removes the squeeze risk - the 4 June put was barely underwater at any point - at the cost of one session's premium.

Do not short a parabola's first red day; wait for the retest that fails on record volume.

Third, expensive volatility is not, by itself, a reason to complicate the structure. A single 24-delta put with 44 days to expiry, bought with thirty-day implied volatility near 141 per cent, sufficed, because a 62 per cent decline in the underlying overwhelmed the expensive premium. On a confirmed parabola reversal, no spread overlay appears anywhere in this history.

Evidence boundary

Evidence notes

The subject is a completed move, reconstructed after it ended. The name owes its selection to the move itself; nothing here measures how often similar setups resolve badly. Specific caveats. The fills assume end-of-day chain marks with the spread crossed in full; a real fill would likely have been better. The exit bid of 13.10 sat below the intrinsic value of 13.32 with one to two days to expiry; holding to expiry or exercising would have yielded roughly 13.3.

The listing constrained the exit. With no August monthly, the July expiry forced the exit on 16 July at a spot of 11.68; the tail of the move, to 10.92 by 24 July, was not captured, and the put did not cover the 3 August earnings. The substance of the 3 June news headline is not established: shorting against a fresh bullish story carries continuation risk, removed only by the fact that the gap was rejected. The insider sales carry February and March dates, not May - weak, corroborating evidence, not the signal. The catalysts of the April leg (16 April, 21-22 April, on volumes of 84-88 million shares) are unidentified; dated news in this record starts only on 7 May 2026. The Wolfspeed lawsuit of 9-11 July came after most of the decline and forms no part of the signal. Finally, the volatility signature for NVTS uses per-date readings.
