POET - historical reconstruction, 30 Mar to 14 May 2026

POET, 30 March to 14 May 2026. A reconstruction of a base breakout that turned parabolic in five sessions, assembled from dated exchange records and end-of-day option quotations.

Type
Reconstruction
Window
30 Mar - 14 May 2026
Instrument
POET Jun-2026 monthly 10 call
Fills basis
End-of-day chains, crossed

Over the first leg of the window POET rose 304.9 per cent on adjusted closes, from 5.08 on 30 March to 20.57 on 14 May; it then fell 66.5 per cent, to 6.89, by 24 July. This note examines the breakout week - which parts of it carried a date before they happened, how the market quoted one defined-risk call through it, and why the reconstruction leaves on the fifth day.

Observed before

What was knowable beforehand

Every element of the entry case carries a date on or before 20 April 2026, the session the reconstruction uses as its entry point. The bounds of the base - closes 4.87-7.44, intraday high 8.27 of 12 March - stood public. So were the accumulation day of 10 April (+15.8 per cent on 18.2 million shares) and the two sessions of 16 and 17 April that held the range top. The 20 April close above the whole range is the signal; in an end-of-day replay, the close of the breakout session is the trigger.

The volatility record carried a second, quieter fact. On 17 April the recorded one-month at-the-money implied volatility stood at 91 per cent against a 200-day mean of 95 per cent: implied volatility had not yet inflated, and options were purchasable at baseline volatility. By 24 April the one-month measure read 153 per cent. A dated event sat further along the track - first-quarter earnings in mid-May, in fact 14 May - which argued for the back month. The classification is stated honestly: the dated record used here identifies no news catalyst under the breakout (dated news in this record starts on 8 May), so the episode is a structure break, not a dated catalyst.

Observed after

What happened

Through March and April the stock sat in a base: closes clamped between 4.87 and 7.44, with an intraday range high of 8.27 printed on 12 March. On 10 April it rose 15.8 per cent to close at 7.04 on 18.2 million shares, roughly two and a half times the average - a first accumulation day. On 16 and 17 April, closes of 7.34 and 7.26 held the top of the range. The signal day, 20 April: up 18.3 per cent to close at 8.59, with a session high of 9.38 on 45.1 million shares, four to six times the norm - a close above the entire March-April range, including the 8.27 intraday high.

What followed ran parabolic: four up days out of five sessions - 23 April was a down day, −8.2 per cent - for a cumulative +108 per cent over the five sessions, on 78.9 million shares on 24 April. On 27 April the stock fell 47 per cent on 199 million shares. The second leg of the window then carried the stock to 20.57 by 14 May, before the decline to 6.89 by 24 July.

How a defined-risk expression behaved

The study takes a single long POET June-2026 monthly 10 call: the back month, sixty days out and spanning the May report, and the first round out-of-the-money strike above the breakout, actually listed. A total loss here means the premium, nothing further.

Ask in full on the way in, bid in full on the way out. At entry the spread measured 5.3 per cent of mid, with the chain spot of 8.59 identical to the adjusted close: a liquid, unstale market. At exit the bid of 5.50 sat above the intrinsic value of 4.91, with the chain spot at 14.91 against an adjusted close of 15.10. The exit trigger was mechanical, a blow-off signature read off dated figures: four up days out of five sessions, +108 per cent cumulatively, the one-month implied volatility at 153 against a 200-day mean of 97, and the term structure inverted - 153 in the front month against 135 and 129 further out.

Interpretation

The discipline

The episode reduces to two rules. The first is to buy the breakout while implied volatility is still at baseline - 91 per cent against a 200-day mean of 95 - rather than after the crowd has repriced it. The second is to leave on the volatility signature, mechanically, into the parabola rather than after it. The cost of hesitation is dated and exact: holding one further session, through the −47 per cent collapse of 27 April, brought the same call back to a bid of 0.90 against the 0.96 entry - breakeven, after a move that had quoted 5.50 three sessions earlier.

When the crash is already in the premium, the put is not the expression.

The third observation concerns the down-leg that followed - the less comfortable one. The facts around the second leg came dated and rich: securities-fraud suits announced between 8 and 13 May, before the second pump; the 14 May report with front-month implied volatility at 348 per cent; a distribution day on 15 May. Yet long puts at implied volatilities of 138 to 168 per cent collected barely anything for being right: the October 14 put, offered at 4.95, bid only 6.80 while the stock halved, and deep in-the-money puts bid below intrinsic value with borrow at 22.7 per cent. The decline already sat in the premium. The method expresses a post-blow-off down-leg as an equity short with a defined stop - here, above 17.17, a level the path never touched - or skips it. Buying puts at three-digit implied volatility does not express it.

Evidence boundary

Evidence notes

The reconstruction concerns a move that had finished before the study began. Selection rests on the move having occurred; the frequency with which comparable setups resolve the other way is not measured. The exit of 24 April landed, in the event, on the exact top close before the −47 per cent session of 27 April. The exit rule is mechanical - parabola plus volatility inversion - but the note admits the timing-luck component without qualification: an exit on 23 April, the down day, would have dealt near a 4.30 bid on the 12-strike equivalent.

Further caveats. The chain's exit spot of 14.91 stands against an adjusted close of 15.10, a snapshot difference of −1.3 per cent; the fills are real quotations, not derived from spot. The cause of the 27 April collapse - 47 per cent on 199 million shares - is not identified from dated records before 8 May; the securities-fraud class actions filed later almost certainly cover this period, but the root cause is not established from dated records. The entry is the close of the signal day, with the stock already up 18 per cent intraday; a conservative entry on 21 April at a spot of 10.25 would have dealt worse. No August or September expiries existed on 18 May - after July the board ran straight to October - which constrained the down-leg expression discussed above. Finally, the recorded earnings surprise of −100 per cent for 14 May sits alongside a revenue surprise of +101 per cent; the pairing is doubtful for a microcap with a near-zero base.
