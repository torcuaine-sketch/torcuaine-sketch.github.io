ALAB - historical reconstruction, 30 Mar to 30 Jun 2026

Astera Labs (ALAB), 30 March to 30 June 2026. A reconstruction of a base break in an AI-infrastructure leader that had halved from its peak - assembled from dated exchange records and end-of-day option quotations.

Type
Reconstruction
Window
30 Mar - 30 Jun 2026
Instrument
ALAB Jun-2026 monthly 160 call
Fills basis
End-of-day chains, crossed

Over the window ALAB rose 381.7 per cent on adjusted closes, from 100.27 to 483.02. This note examines which parts of that move carried a date before it happened, and how the market quoted one defined-risk option structure through it.

Observed before

What was knowable beforehand

Every element of the entry case predates the 10 April 2026 entry. The earnings record showed two consecutive dated beats: 4 November 2025 (earnings +25.7 per cent, revenue +11.7 per cent) and 10 February 2026 (earnings +12.4 per cent, revenue +8.4 per cent). The structure sat in the tape: the September 2025 peak of 251.88, the four-week March base of 107-135.18, the 30 March undercut to 97.89 (close 100.27) and the three-day reclaim to 117.14 on 2 April. The entry signal itself: the 10 April close at 149.05, +15.1 per cent on the day, above the whole base, on 2.3 times average volume; in an end-of-day replay, the breakout close is the signal.

The volatility signature carried dates and pointed the same way. The 30-day implied volatility rose with the price - 0.880 on 30 March, 0.908 on 8 April, 0.968 on 10 April - and from 6 April the 30-day measure sat above the earnings-excluded 30-day measure (0.876 against 0.806): the market was already pricing a dated earnings event inside thirty days. Against the November/February reporting cadence, that placed the report in early May. It in fact arrived on 5 May.

Two honesty notes belong in this section. The break was not a 52-week high - the February high of 187.67 stood above it - and the specific news behind the +15 per cent day cannot be established from dated records. The classification rests on price, volume and volatility, not on a news item.

Observed after

What happened

The stock had peaked at 251.88 on 18 September 2025 and then roughly halved. Through March it built a four-week base between 107 and 135.18. On 30 March it undercut the base, flushing to 97.89 and closing at 100.27, then reclaimed the range within three days: a close of 117.14 on 2 April, up 10.4 per cent. 10 April supplied the signal session: a close of 149.05, up 15.1 per cent on the day, above the entire March base (whose high of 135.18 dated from 17 March), on 10.9 million shares - about 2.3 times the average volume.

The dated catalyst: the early-May report, arriving on 5 May with another earnings beat, +13.6 per cent. The advance continued to the 18 June expiry - the study closed the structure that day at a spot of 415.73 - and on to a close of 483.02 on 30 June, the boundary of the study window. The following month the stock fell back to 291.

How a defined-risk expression behaved

The study takes a single long ALAB June-2026 monthly 160 call - slightly out of the money at entry, a back month spanning the dated early-May report and the runway after it. The most such a structure can lose is the premium paid, and nothing more.

With the spot at 415.73, intrinsic value stood at 255.73 and the bid of 251.50 sat at 98.3 per cent of intrinsic - a normal deep-in-the-money market, and the conservative side of it; an exercise would have captured the full intrinsic value. The expiry, not a signal, set the exit.

Interpretation

The discipline

The episode illustrates a hold configuration, not a fade: a multi-week base break in a leader that had fallen fifty per cent from its peak, with implied volatility rising together with the price and a dated report three to four weeks away. The expression is a back-month call through the event, not a front week. Rising-with-price implied volatility reads as a move that is not finished; paying a high volatility - the 30-day measure ran near 97 per cent at entry - is defensible only at exactly this confluence of structure and a dated catalyst.

Let the expiry carry the exit discipline. The June contract spanned the trend and, by ending, mechanically prevented overstaying the July slide from 483 to 291.

The second point: what the instrument’s finiteness did. The June expiry captured a near-tripling of the underlying from the 150.15 entry spot to the 415.73 exit spot, and then stopped existing. A longer instrument would have kept the position alive into the July collapse. The choice of expiry was, in effect, the exit rule - written before the position existed, which is where exit rules belong.

Evidence boundary

Evidence notes

The entry is the end-of-day ask of the signal session, on the convention that the breakout close is the signal; a strict next-day entry on 13 April, at a spot of 166.8, would have entered roughly 11 per cent higher in the underlying. The news driver of the +15 per cent session of 10 April is not identifiable from dated records, and the classification rests on price, volume and volatility rather than a headline.

Specific caveats. The June call does not capture the full +382 per cent of the window, to 483.02 on 30 June; a July-2026 call would additionally have spanned the Nasdaq-100 inclusion (announced 12 June, effective 22 June) and the 29-30 June blow-off, but would have tempted overstaying the July slide to 291 - the trade-off is noted, not resolved. The exit took a bid of 251.50 against an intrinsic value of 255.73; the record keeps the conservative side. Mid-trade, the record shows the chief operating officer selling roughly $3 million of stock at prices of 193-213 on 7 May (filing of 11 May) - a pressure fact a holder would have seen and had to absorb. The entry spread of 4.4 per cent of mid leaves no staleness concern.
