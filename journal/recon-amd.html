AMD - historical reconstruction, 30 Mar to 30 Jun 2026

Advanced Micro Devices (AMD), 30 March to 30 June 2026. A reconstruction of a megacap range break in which implied volatility rose with the price - assembled from dated exchange records and end-of-day option quotations.

Type
Reconstruction
Window
30 Mar - 30 Jun 2026
Instrument
AMD Jun-2026 monthly 250 call
Fills basis
End-of-day chains, crossed

Over the window AMD rose 196.3 per cent on adjusted closes, from 196.04 to 580.91. Which parts of the move were datable in advance - and how the market quoted one defined-risk option structure through it - is the note's subject.

Observed before

What was knowable beforehand

Every element of the entry case predates the 9 April 2026 entry. The structure: the double bottom of 13 March (low 192.27) and 30 March (low 192.87), then the 8 April gap close at 231.82 above the 25 March swing high of 221.33, on 35.5 million shares. The entry comes strictly after the signal - the day after the breakout close, not at the low of the window.

The volatility signature carried dates in plain view. From the historical option-chain summaries, 30-day implied volatility ran 0.512 on 25 March, 0.577 on 8 April and 0.580 on 9 April, while the spot rose from 220 to 237 - implied volatility rising together with price, with the front over the back (the 30-day measure above the 90-day). Call demand expanded into the breakout rather than meeting sellers.

The catalyst carried a date. Earnings sat on the schedule for 5 May 2026, inside the life of the instrument, and the previous report of 3 February 2026 had shown an earnings surprise of +15.9 per cent with revenue up 6.2 per cent - a beat trend already established in the record.

Observed after

What happened

The stock built a double bottom through March: a low of 192.27 on 13 March and a low of 192.87 on 30 March. On 8 April it gapped to a close of 231.82, above the swing high of 221.33 set on 25 March - a break of the entire February-March range, on 35.5 million shares. The reconstruction enters the following session, 9 April, at a chain spot of 236.79.

The dated catalyst: the 5 May report, which produced an 18.6 per cent gap. The trend continued through the window: from 237 at entry to 513 by 17 June, the session the structure closed, one trading day before the 18 June expiry. The stock went on to close the window at 580.91 on 30 June - a stretch the studied instrument, by construction, did not span.

How a defined-risk expression behaved

The study takes a single long AMD June-2026 monthly 250 call - 5.6 per cent out of the money at entry, a back month covering the 5 May report with a month of spare runway. Losses on the structure stop at the premium paid.

Both fills take the full quotation - the ask at entry, the bid at exit. The entry spread measured 1.7 per cent of mid: liquid, not stale. At exit the contract was deep in the money; with the spot at 513.01, intrinsic value was 263.01 and the bid of 261.80 sat roughly 1.2 below parity - a normal, dealable deep-in-the-money market. The exit came one trading day before expiry, at intrinsic value; the position never needed an exit signal.

Interpretation

The discipline

The episode illustrates a specific confluence: a break of a multi-week range in a megacap, implied volatility rising with the price rather than collapsing into the rally, and a dated report inside the horizon. The expression that fits is a back-month near-the-money call - about 70 days to expiry, expiring after the event - not the front month. A single 71-day instrument carried the 18.6 per cent earnings gap of 5 May and the entire trend from 237 to 513 with no gamma bleed and no re-entry.

When implied volatility rises with the price, buyers are taking the breakout, not fading it - and the wing is the instrument, not something to be capped with a spread.

The second point: structural simplicity. No spread overlay appears in this history, and none proved necessary: the uncapped call expressed the thesis. The exit was equally unglamorous - expiry approached, the contract sat at its intrinsic value, and the quotation went at the bid. Discipline here consisted of choosing the right expiry once, and then doing nothing.

Evidence boundary

Evidence notes

This page reconstructs a move that had already happened. Selection followed the outcome - the move put the name under study - and the page offers no estimate of how often comparable setups resolve badly. The call expired on 18 June: the tail of the window, 18 to 30 June (537 to 581, roughly +8 per cent in the underlying), is not captured without a roll and is not counted.

Specific caveats. The 24 April gap (+14 per cent on 81.6 million shares) has no AMD-specific dated precursor in the news reviewed here, and the case does not rest on knowing that news in advance. Guidance direction is unrecorded for AMD across the period, a known gap in the evidence. The chain summaries spot for 30 March of 194.3 differs from the adjusted close of 196.04 - a 15:45 snapshot against the close, immaterial. The volatility signature comes from the daily option summaries. The entry and exit quotations themselves are tight on both sides - a near-the-money entry spread of 1.7 per cent of mid and an intrinsic-value exit - which excludes staleness at either end.
